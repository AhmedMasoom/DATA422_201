6. Meeting Cadence
•	Weekly Internal team meeting: Every Friday at 8:30 pm, approx. 45 minutes for reviewing progress, planning the week, unblocking the issues.
•	Weekly meeting with Mentor: Ever Sunday, approximately 1 hour for reviewing the progress
•	Short mid-week check-in: Async update in WhatsApp / ClickUp
•	Each meeting has a brief agenda; the Project Lead records key decisions and action items in ClickUp.
•	Ad-hoc working sessions as needed near deliverable dates.

7. Decision-Making
•	Routine decisions are made by the relevant role lead.
•	Decisions affecting scope, methods or deliverables are made by the team, aiming for consensus.
•	If consensus cannot be reached, we decide by majority vote; the Project Lead breaks a tie.
•	All significant decisions are recorded (in ClickUp or meeting notes) so they are not relitigated.

8. Ways of Working – Team Norms
•	Treat each other with respect; everyone’s input is valued.
•	Keep tasks in ClickUp up to date – status, blockers and links to outputs.
•	Deliver your tasks by the agreed date, or flag early if you cannot.
•	Give feedback on the work, not the person; assume good intent.
•	Version control: save files in the shared drive with clear names; avoid duplicate copies.
•	Reference everything – capture sources as you go for citations.

9. Task & Project Management
We manage the project in ClickUp using the imported plan (8 phases, 49 subtasks). Conventions:
•	Every task has an owner, a due date and a status (To Do / In Progress / In Review / On Hold / Done).
•	Update your task status before each meeting.
•	Use the Board view for standups, Gantt/Timeline to watch the critical path, and Calendar for the week.
•	Raise blockers as ClickUp comments and @mention the people involved.






10. Accountability & Deadlines
•	Internal deadlines are set a few days before any official due date to allow review and buffer.
•	If a member repeatedly misses commitments, the team discusses it openly and supportively first.
•	Persistent issues are escalated to the program tutor/coordinator as a last resort.
•	We track contribution fairly, so peer/teamwork assessment reflects reality.

11. Quality Standards – Definition of Done
We will be assessed against the brief’s six rubric criteria. A deliverable is “done” when it meets these:
Rubric criterion	What “good” looks like for us
Written Communication / Structure	Clear, well-organised report within 2,500–3,000 words, proofread.
Getting Information	Relevant, credible data sources identified and documented.
Analysing Data & Information	Sound, reproducible analysis; conclusions supported by evidence.
Critical Thinking	Honest about limitations; thoughtful recommendations and next steps.
Teamwork	Equitable contribution; decisions and workload shared.
Presentation Skills	Clear, well-rehearsed 10-minute talk; everyone presents.
Every report section and the deck get a peer review before submission.

12. Conflict Resolution
1.	Raise the issue early and directly with the people involved, in private and respectfully.
2.	If unresolved, bring it to the full team to discuss and agree a way forward.
3.	If still unresolved, the Project Lead facilitates a decision per Section 7.
4.	As a last resort, escalate to the program tutor/coordinator.
